# Weak signals

A weak signal is an early, low-volume piece of information that may announce a bigger
change (the notion comes from strategic management: Ansoff, 1975, "Managing strategic
surprise by response to weak signals"). In customer feedback, it is a topic that is
**rare today but would be costly to ignore**.

## Detection criteria (at least one)

1. **Novelty** - appears only in the most recent period (compare first vs last months).
2. **Growth** - small in volume but rising period after period.
3. **Severity** - safety, health, legal, data protection, fraud, discrimination.
4. **Concentration** - same issue on one product, batch, carrier, store or agent.
5. **Influence** - mention of social media, press, consumer associations, regulators.
6. **New expectation** - customers asking for something the brand does not offer yet
   (refill packs, a new payment method, a new channel). This can be an opportunity.

## How to report one

- `signal`: short, factual title ("Skin reactions reported on the new night cream").
- `count` and `evidence_ids`: every verbatim id that supports it.
- `why_it_matters`: the risk or opportunity if it grows.
- `recommended_check`: a concrete verification (check the batch, call 3 customers, monitor
  next month, add a tag in the ticketing tool).

Never inflate a weak signal into a trend: state the volume plainly.
